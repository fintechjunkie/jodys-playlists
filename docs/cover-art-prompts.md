# Cover Art Prompts — Jody's Playlists

The covers have to do two contradictory jobs: look like a **family** (so a row of
them reads as one collection) and be **instantly distinguishable** from each
other at thumbnail size in a Spotify sidebar or an ad slot.

The way to get both is to freeze everything except two variables:

- **CONSTANT:** ground color, texture, treatment, composition logic, palette
- **VARIABLE:** the motif (one object), and which brand color it's printed in

That's it. Same print process, different subject. It's how a record label's
sleeve series works.

---

## The family style block

This paragraph goes at the front of **every** prompt, unchanged. Only the
bracketed parts get swapped.

> Flat two-color screenprint album cover, square 1:1 composition. Warm
> off-white paper ground, hex #FFF8F6, with visible paper grain and a fine
> halftone dot texture. A single oversized **[MOTIF]** rendered as a flat
> cut-paper silhouette in **[ACCENT]** — solid fill, no outline, no gradient,
> no shading, no interior detail. The shape is cropped generously so it bleeds
> off at least two edges of the frame. Deep forest green, hex #00522D, appears
> only once, as a small secondary shape or a thin line detail. Slight
> offset-print misregistration, a 1–2mm color shift at one edge. Bold, graphic,
> editorial risograph poster aesthetic. High contrast. Must read clearly at
> thumbnail size.

**Negative prompt (append to all):**

> no text, no letters, no words, no typography, no logos, no watermark, no
> photography, no 3D render, no drop shadow, no glow, no lens flare, no
> gradient, no bevel, no photorealism, no clutter, no busy background, no
> border, no frame

---

## The palette

Only these. Nothing else, ever.

| Color | Hex | Use as accent |
|---|---|---|
| Lipstick Magenta | `#DB3C8A` | The loud default |
| Cotton Pink | `#E878B2` | Softer, mid-tone |
| Bubblegum | `#F29EBD` | Quietest, for gentle playlists |
| Forest Ink | `#00522D` | Grounded, serious, nocturnal |
| Blush Cream | `#FCE5DF` | Whisper — only for very calm playlists |

Ground is always `#FFF8F6`. Forest green always appears as the small second
color, even when it's also the accent (then use magenta as the small detail).

## PROMPT 1 — Halloween Party, Actually Scary

The whole positioning is "not campy." So: no pumpkins, no bats, no cobwebs, no
orange. An elegant, wrong, slightly biological motif does the work instead.

> appears only once, as a small secondary shape or a thin line detail. Slight
> offset-print misregistration, a 1–2mm color shift at one edge. Bold, graphic,
> editorial risograph poster aesthetic. High contrast. Must read clearly at
> thumbnail size.
>
> no text, no letters, no words, no typography, no logos, no watermark, no
> photography, no 3D render, no drop shadow, no glow, no lens flare, no

**Alternate motifs if the moth doesn't land:** a single bare branch with no
leaves · an open doorway with nothing visible through it · a hand with fingers
spread, palm out · a crescent moon partially eclipsed.

## Accent order across the index

Rule 6 below says vary the accent between neighbors. With all five playlists the
index currently alternates cleanly, which is why "Day One Of No Contact" is green
rather than the softer pink you might expect:

| # | Playlist | Motif color |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Halloween Party, Actually Scary | forest green |
| 2 | Revenge Dressing | lipstick magenta |
| 3 | The 47 Minute Rage Clean | forest green |
| 4 | Feel Seventeen Again | cotton pink |
| 5 | Day One Of No Contact | forest green |

No two neighbors share a color. If you reorder the playlists, re-check this.

## Rules for inventing new covers

1. **One object. Always one.** Two objects and the thumbnail turns to mud.
2. **Pick a noun, not a mood.** "Melancholy" gives you nothing; "a burnt match"
   gives you a cover.
3. **Crop hard.** The shape should feel too big for the frame. Timid, centered,
   fully-visible objects look like clip art.
4. **The motif should be an object from the scene the playlist describes** — the
   thing that would actually be in the room. That's what makes it feel authored
   rather than decorative.
5. **Never reuse a motif** across playlists, and don't use two motifs from the
   same category (two flowers, two shoes) unless the playlists are a deliberate
   pair.
6. **Vary the accent color between neighbors** in the index, the same way the
   title lines alternate on the site.

## Export

- **Square, 3000×3000**, JPEG.
- Spotify compresses covers hard, so **avoid fine detail and thin lines** — the
  halftone texture should be coarse enough to survive.
- **Test every cover at 64px** before committing to it. If you can't tell it
  apart from another cover in the set at that size, change the accent color or
  the motif.
- Save into `public/covers/<slug>.jpg`, then set `cover: "/covers/<slug>.jpg"`
  in `lib/playlists.ts`.
- Also set `accent` in that same entry to **[the motif color, the ground
  color]** — it's the flat color shown behind the image while it loads, so
  matching the artwork keeps the page from flashing.

## One deliberate choice: no type on the covers

The covers carry no text. Two reasons: image generators render lettering badly,
and the site already sets every title in huge condensed display type directly
beside the cover — repeating it inside the image just competes with itself.

If you want type on the covers for Spotify (where there's no surrounding
layout), add it afterward in a real design tool using **Anton**, uppercase,
tight leading, in the motif's color. Don't ask the generator to do it.
